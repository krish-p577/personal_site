GPT6-Astra came out a few days ago. It's really made me wonder what the end goal for LLM development actually is. 

I want to draw some predictions and thoughts I had on how AI is going to affect humanity beyond software, how its going to change society and culture, and what we should look out for.

I've realized that its pretty hard to make predictions on the behavior of LLMs on a large scale, especially given how unpredictable LLM development has been. We see models getting better and more integrated with our workflows. At the same time seeing frontier labs getting involved in circular financing and shady economics. Its hard to predict how LLMs will be involved in our lives even 6 months from now. 

That doesn't give you much ground to stand on your predictions of the impact of LLMs, especially on the large scale. But that's not going to stop me. 


Society.

Recently, the mayor of New York City, Zohran Mamdani has suspended the use of AI in classrooms up to grade 8 by a year. An important decision that I hope more cities and school boards will follow. 

We don't know the long term effects of consistent LLM usage on people, but we can make some pretty confident predictions. 
You might have heard the term cognitive offloading recently. Its when people shift their thinking from their brains to their favorite LLMs. Letting the LLM do the thinking for them. Thus reducing their own ability to think through an overdependence. 

My main concern within the effects of cognitive offloading, is shifting one's decision making away from oneself. 

Talked to a friend recently where we were discussing between two options for him to take, and almost immediately he throws them into ChatGPT for an answer. 
There's nothing wrong with doing research or trying to get all the facts before you make a big decision, but something about using an LLM to decide what's right for you bugs me. 

Think long term, when we outsource our decision making, we don't comprehend all the factors that go into a decision, we trust the LLM to weigh our options for us. At some point, we might not even consider things like, "is this something I want" or "is this what's best for me". Like children who trust their parents blindly, it might become very easy to give up our personal autonomy to LLMs. 


Which leaves you open to being influenced by whatever motives that might be behind the people behind those LLMs. Through things like advertisements or propaganda, pushing millions of people towards buying certain products, or supporting certain political parties. I don't think its outrageous to consider these possibilities, given some contracts frontier labs have already signed. 

I think its important that we maintain our decision making abilities for ourselves. Because at the end of the day we are the only ones accountable for our choices.



Culture. 

It's harder to speak on the cultural effects of LLMs, just because predicting changes in culture is always difficult.

I think one of the biggest cultural impacts will be through media. What happens when the slop becomes as good as the real thing? 

Many artists you'll hear from will report their disdain for the use of LLMs in generating things like paintings, movies, music, or anything that usually needs human creativity/skill to create. But it seems inevitable that we are going to start seeing AI be used to help with the creation of media. From large and small studios/teams alike, using AI to increase efficiency, save costs, and achieve things they may not have been able to prior. 


For example, what happens when LLM video becomes so good that anyone can make blockbuster level movies for 0.001% of the traditional cost? 
It levels the playing field, anyone with an idea can create a full length feature film with the likes of Robert Pattinson and the cinematography of a Nolan film. 
Some might rejoice in the freedom this gives the masses, to create what they want without facing the reality of production.

But does this actually improve the quality of films being created? Or does it just mean that there are just more bad movies that now take up space in theaters. 

When everyone is able to make high-budget level films and every movie is able to have the most impressive visuals, are they still needed to make a good movie?


I think with LLMs making visuals cheap to produce, that visuals in film will become mostly unimportant, or at least, the quality of the visuals won't matter anymore. 

This would change the way we use visuals in media, maybe a shift away from realism and into abstract imagery. Where details are less important and ideas/feelings take the center state.

The landscape around AI changes every 3 months, its hard to know what it may look like in the future. But I'm excited to come back to this in a year and see how it shapes up.
